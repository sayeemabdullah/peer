# Field Layer: Computational Social Science

Covers social science conducted with large-scale digital data: platform
data, digital traces, text-as-data, simulation of social processes, and
computational analysis of human behavior. Inherits the causal-inference
discipline in `social-science.md` — read that for identification strategy —
with additional problems specific to found data at scale.

## Found data is not a sample of a population

The data was generated by a platform for its own purposes, not sampled for
your question. That has consequences that large N does not fix:
- **Platform users are not the general population**, and differ by
  demographics, geography, and political orientation in ways that vary by
  platform and over time.
- **Active users are not users**: a small fraction of accounts produce most
  content, so content-weighted analyses describe the most active minority.
- **Bots, spam, duplicates, and organizational accounts** are present and
  must be handled explicitly; the handling method changes results.
- **Platform algorithms mediate what is observed** — visibility, recommendation,
  and moderation shape the trace data, so measured "behavior" is partly the
  algorithm's behavior. This confound is often unaddressable and should be
  stated rather than ignored.
- Large N shrinks standard errors without touching any of this bias. A tight
  confidence interval around a biased estimate is precisely wrong.
  Flag when large N is being treated as if it conferred representativeness.

## API and sampling mechanics

- Which endpoint, what sampling rate, what date range, and what query terms?
  Streaming samples, keyword-filtered collections, and snowball samples each
  have distinct biases.
- Keyword-based collection defines the population by the keyword list;
  report the list and consider its blind spots.
- Data collected at different times isn't comparable if the platform changed
  its API, algorithm, or moderation policy in between — a real confound for
  longitudinal claims.
- **Deletion and retrospective collection**: content deleted before
  collection is systematically missing, and it is not missing at random
  (moderated, deleted-by-author, suspended accounts).

## Text-as-data and measurement

- Dictionary methods, supervised classifiers, and topic models all produce
  measurements with error. Validate against human-coded ground truth on a
  sample, report agreement, and — critically — account for classifier error
  when using predictions as a variable in a downstream analysis, since
  ignoring it biases estimates.
- Topic models require the interpretation of topics to be validated, not
  assigned by the authors' reading alone.
- Sentiment and stance classifiers trained on one domain transfer poorly to
  another; report in-domain validation.
- Where an LLM is used for annotation, treat it as a measurement instrument
  requiring validation against human labels, with agreement reported
  (see `artificial-intelligence.md`).

## Simulation and agent-based models

- State the assumptions, parameter values, and their sources. A model
  calibrated to reproduce a stylized fact does not thereby explain it.
- Sensitivity analysis across the plausible parameter range is expected;
  results shown only at one parameter setting are not characterized.
- Distinguish what the simulation demonstrates (this mechanism *can*
  produce this pattern) from what it does not (this mechanism *did*).

## Ethics

- Public availability is not consent. Analysis of public posts can still
  harm identifiable individuals, particularly for sensitive topics
  (health, sexuality, immigration status, political dissent under
  authoritarian governments).
- IRB review applies; "public data" exemptions should be determined by the
  board, not assumed.
- Quoting verbatim text can make an author findable through search even in
  an otherwise anonymized dataset — paraphrase or aggregate where the topic
  is sensitive.
- Platform terms of service and data-sharing restrictions constrain what can
  be released; plan for what *can* be shared (IDs for rehydration, derived
  aggregates, code) rather than sharing nothing.

## Publication norms

- Venues: ICWSM, CSCW, WWW, EPJ Data Science, Nature Human Behaviour, PNAS,
  plus disciplinary social science journals.
- Preprints (SocArXiv, arXiv) are standard.
- Code and derived-data release is expected where terms of service permit.

## Red flags

- Large N presented as if it addressed representativeness
- Platform data generalized to "people" or "the public" without qualification
- A classifier's predictions used as a variable with no validation and no
  error correction
- A causal claim from observational trace data with no identification
  strategy
- "Public data, so no ethics review needed"
