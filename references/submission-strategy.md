# Submission Strategy

Covers choosing where and how to submit a finished or near-finished
manuscript: journal fit, preprint decisions, and the cover letter to the
editor.

## Journal fit and scope match

- Ask what journals the user is already considering, and check the fit
  concretely: does the journal's stated scope actually cover this study's
  topic, design, and population, not just its general field? A mismatch
  here is one of the most common (and most easily avoidable) reasons for a
  desk rejection.
- Check the manuscript's current framing against the target journal's
  audience — a paper written for specialists may need a broader framing for
  a general-audience journal, or vice versa. This can mean redrafting parts
  of the introduction/discussion, not just picking a different venue.
- Consider journal tier honestly against the strength of the finding and
  the study's limitations (sample size, replication status, effect size).
  Aiming unrealistically high costs time in review-and-reject cycles;
  aiming low when the work supports more is a real cost too. Give an honest
  read rather than defaulting to encouragement.
- Check practical factors: open access requirements or costs (APCs),
  typical time to first decision, whether the target funder mandates a
  specific licensing/deposit requirement.

## Preprint decisions

- Preprints are broadly accepted across most quantitative fields now
  (arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv, PsyArXiv, SocArXiv, SSRN), but check the target
  journal's policy before assuming it's compatible — a minority of venues
  (some clinical journals in particular) restrict or prohibit prior
  preprint posting, or have embargo rules.
- Preprinting establishes priority and invites early feedback, which can be
  genuinely useful before formal submission — but for clinical findings with
  direct public-health relevance, weigh this against the risk of unreviewed
  findings being acted on or reported publicly before peer review.

## Cover letter to the editor

- State plainly and specifically why this paper fits this journal — not a
  generic statement of importance, but the actual scope match identified
  above.
- Summarize the core contribution in a few sentences, calibrated to the
  claim the manuscript's results actually support (Standing Rule 2 and the
  overclaiming check from `adversarial-review.md` apply here too — a cover
  letter that oversells relative to the paper is a bad first impression).
  If this is a resubmission or the paper was previously reviewed elsewhere,
  say so if the journal requires disclosure, and follow that journal's
  specific policy.
- Note anything the editor needs upfront: conflicts of interest, suggested
  or excluded reviewers (if the venue allows this and the user has grounds
  for the exclusion), related work under review elsewhere.

## Working with the user

If they haven't decided between journals, help them compare 2-3 realistic
options against the criteria above rather than just picking the most
prestigious option by default. If the manuscript isn't actually
submission-ready (missing elements per `reporting-standards.md`, unresolved
issues from `adversarial-review.md`), say so before helping strategize the
submission — venue choice doesn't fix a manuscript that isn't ready.
